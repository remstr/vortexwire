Mark Zuckerberg wants you to wear your AI like a piece of high-tech jewelry, but some of us aren't waiting for the "Meta tax" to hit our wallets. While the official "Muse Charm" aims to be the holiday season's must-have digital pet, one developer just built a $50 DIY version that does the same job without the corporate markup.

Using a 1.75-inch touchscreen devkit drop-shipped from China, this project mirrors Meta’s open-source "Muse Gadget" blueprints. It’s a silver, metallic pocket watch that talks to the cloud—no 5G or creepy built-in cameras required.

- It doubles as a remote viewfinder for your phone’s camera.
- It theoretically controls your smart home, provided you’re willing to inject some "vibe code" into the setup.

Getting it to behave wasn't exactly seamless. Initially, the AI thought it was a transcriptionist rather than an assistant, and the default "Jolly" avatar mostly just sat there looking cute while the text scrolled at a snail's pace.

But for fifty bucks and some quality time with Claude and GitHub, it’s a genuine win. It’s a slightly janky, totally customizable AI pendant that feels more like a hacker’s Tamagotchi than a mass-market accessory.

- Total cost: $50 (Waveshare ESP32-S3 1.75C).
- Brains: Meta’s open-source Muse AI.
- Vibe: Cyberpunk pocket watch.
