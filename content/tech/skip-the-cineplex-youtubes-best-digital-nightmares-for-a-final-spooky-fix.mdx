Hollywood is currently obsessed with three-hour epics, but true terror doesn’t need a director’s cut or a massive marketing budget. If you want to close out the season without committing to a franchise, YouTube’s basement of uncanny shorts is ready to traumatize you.

Start with *Possibly in Michigan*, a 1983 fever dream that plays like a feminist musical directed by a caffeinated David Lynch. It’s twelve minutes of synth-pop and cannibalistic charm that will stay with you longer than most blockbuster jumpscares.

If you prefer your existential dread with a side of "what am I looking at," *Homemade Gatorade* threads the needle between comedy and absolute revulsion. Its stiff, processed animation creates a road trip from hell that makes the "uncanny valley" look like a pleasant vacation spot.

For those who like their horror high-brow and low-visibility, check these out:

- *Portrait of God*: A masterclass in cosmic horror that proves staring into an empty abyss is way scarier than any CGI monster.
- *Heck*: The 29-minute blueprint for *Skinamarink* that delivers all the static-drenched tension in a much more digestible sitting.
- *Dining Room or There is Nothing*: A one-minute loop of pure internet discomfort that defined early viral horror.

You don't need a streaming subscription to trigger a fight-or-flight response. Sometimes, all it takes is a grainy camera and a deeply wrong idea to prove the most effective monsters are the ones lurking in your browser tab.
