Flock Safety, the AI-surveillance giant, is currently acting like that one toxic ex who refuses to take the hint after a breakup. Multiple cities are trying to dump their license plate reader contracts, but Flock is essentially ghosting the cancellation requests and, in some cases, breaking back into the house.

In Plymouth, Minnesota, the city council voted to terminate their contract back in September. After a month of radio silence while the cameras stayed suspiciously active, Police Chief Eric Fadden decided he’d had enough. 

He took matters into his own hands and physically pulled the power cords. It turns out that when a tech company ignores your emails, the "unplug it and see" method works for more than just a frozen router.

- Plymouth PD had to manually disconnect cameras after being ignored for weeks.
- Dayton, MN, saw Flock reinstall hardware after being explicitly told to stop.
- Elgin Community College in Illinois faced a similar "refusal to leave" situation.

Over in Dayton, the gaslighting reached new heights. Despite a direct order to pause all work, Flock technicians reconnected cameras without authorization. 

Flock claims they "respect" these municipal decisions, but their behavior suggests "no" actually means "maybe later." If your city is planning on cutting ties, you might want to keep some wire cutters handy.
