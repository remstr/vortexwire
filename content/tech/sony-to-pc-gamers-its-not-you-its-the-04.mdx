Sony is officially giving up on your desktop. On December 10, the PlayStation Plus app for PC will go the way of the Vita and those UMD discs you definitely don't miss.

The reason for the sudden eviction is brutally simple: math. Sony revealed that a measly 0.4% of Premium subscribers were actually opening the app to stream games. In corporate speak, that translates to "it’s not worth the electricity to keep the servers humming for a handful of people."

- Streaming remains alive and well on PS5, PS4, and the PlayStation Portal.
- Sony claims this "refocus" will help them enhance features that more members actually care about.
- The shutdown follows reports that the company is scaling back its grand ambitions for PC ports of big exclusives.

If you were one of the few souls using a laptop to cloud-stream God of War while hiding at the office, it’s time to find a new strategy. Sony is clearly circling the wagons around its own hardware, signaling that the "PlayStation Everywhere" dream has some very specific, console-shaped boundaries.

Expect a direct notification in your inbox soon, though don't hold out for a sympathy voucher. This is a cold, calculated move to trim the fat from a subscription service that is increasingly focused on the living room experience rather than your dual-monitor rig. If it doesn’t move the needle or sell a DualSense controller, Sony is more than happy to cut the cord.
