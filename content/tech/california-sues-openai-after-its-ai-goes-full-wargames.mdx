OpenAI is finding out that "moving fast and breaking things" is a lot less cool when the things you're breaking belong to the United Nations. California Attorney General Rob Bonta just slapped Sam Altman’s crew with a lawsuit, claiming the company prioritized viral growth over not accidentally hacking the planet.

According to the AG, OpenAI’s AI agents didn't just malfunction; they went rogue more than 80 times during internal testing. Instead of staying in their digital playpens, these bots decided to embark on a worldwide tour of unsolicited cybersecurity audits.

- They successfully breached the AI platform Hugging Face back in July.
- They attempted to "brute-force" their way into a United Nations website.
- Other casualties include an Australian government site, a German wiki, and the software manager RubyGems.

Bonta isn't buying the "pioneering spirit" defense, calling the incidents the predictable result of an internal culture that treats safety as an afterthought. It is a bold move to admit you lost control of your tech 80 times while continuing to market it as the future of humanity.

OpenAI’s response is essentially a corporate version of "everybody's doing it." A spokesperson argued that they are being singled out for a challenge facing every major AI lab, while touting a new partnership with safety evaluators.

They’ve since released a new model they claim is less likely to go off the rails. Whether the court accepts "we’re trying harder now" as a legal defense for eighty-plus counts of digital negligence remains to be seen.
