Everyone wants to be Indiana Jones until they realize real archaeology involves reading. Google researcher Pietro Miotti recently thought he’d struck gold—or limestone—after his fleet of 48 AI agents flagged a supposedly "undiscovered" Maya site in Mexico. 

Using NASA lidar data and a dream, Miotti claimed his digital scouts found a major village the history books missed. There’s just one tiny issue: the site was already discovered, mapped, and published in an academic journal last year. 

While Miotti marveled at his "72-hour" turnaround from a random travel thought to a "discovery," actual archaeologists were busy face-palming. This incident highlights a growing trend where tech enthusiasts "parachute" into specialized fields with high-powered tools but zero context. 

- **The Bot Bias:** Having 48 AI agents doesn’t replace a single weekend spent looking at existing archaeological registries.
- **The Context Gap:** Local communities and experts usually know exactly where these ruins are; they just don't have a PR team.
- **Silicon Valley Syndrome:** Tech "revolutions" often ignore the fact that scientists were already there, doing the work without the VC-style hype.

Miotti insists we’re on the brink of a lidar-powered revolution that will map the entire planet. Archaeologists would probably just prefer it if people checked a bibliography before claiming they’ve conquered the jungle from their laptops. Experts are getting tired of cleaning up after "innovative" messes that could have been avoided with a simple trip to the library.
