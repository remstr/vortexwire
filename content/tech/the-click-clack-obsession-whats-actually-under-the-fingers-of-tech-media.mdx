Let’s talk about the original brain-computer interface. No, not a Neuralink chip—just the slab of plastic you’re currently using to doomscroll or argue with strangers. The Verge recently peeked under the hoods of its own writers' desks, and the results range from "ergonomic survivalist" to "I bought this solely for the vibes."

Most tech writers fall into two camps: those who treat their peripherals like specialized medical equipment and those who treat them like high-end home decor. 

- The Ergo-Warriors: The Kinesis Freestyle Pro is a favorite for the carpal tunnel crowd, offering a split design that looks like a keyboard mid-divorce but saves your tendons.
- The Clicky-Clackers: Keychron and NuPhy are the go-to brands for anyone who wants a mechanical thwack satisfying enough to make a coworker twitch.
- The Vibe-Checkers: The Logitech G713 is a sleeper hit, mostly because it looks sleek and comes with a puffy wrist rest that feels like typing on a literal marshmallow.

It turns out that even the most seasoned tech experts aren't immune to "shiny object" syndrome. While some hunt for adjustable actuation points and linear red switches, others are perfectly happy with the flat, soul-crushing Apple Magic Keyboard because it fits the desk aesthetic.

Your keyboard is your most intimate work partner. Whether it’s a $300 custom build or the membrane board that came free with your PC, it’s the only thing standing between your brain and the digital abyss.
