Donald Trump touched down in the Philippines this week to a greeting of five beauty queens and a brass band rendition of "YMCA." It’s a familiar aesthetic for a very different kind of land grab: a 4,000-acre industrial hub dedicated to AI and microchips.

Since Americans are currently revolting against massive data centers and chip fabs appearing in their own zip codes, the administration is exporting the noise and power bills overseas. Enter "Pax Silica," the US-led effort to secure the minerals and hardware needed to keep the AI hype cycle spinning.

The Philippine government is rolling out the red carpet, but the locals aren’t exactly buying the "peace and prosperity" pitch. While officials dream of tech riches, protesters are lining the streets with prop caskets to mourn the inevitable loss of land, homes, and natural resources.

Here is what’s actually on the table:
- A massive 1,620-hectare project designed to house chip factories and critical mineral processing plants.
- An international coalition of two dozen countries signed up to help the US "win" the AI race by hosting the infrastructure no one wants at home.

Trump’s pitch is simple: the US gets the GPUs, and the Philippines gets a slice of the generative AI pie. Whether that pie is worth 4,000 acres of razed land is a question the Philippine public is currently answering with a resounding "hard pass."
