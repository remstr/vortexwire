SpaceX just turned high-stakes orbital engineering into a giant game of Operation. At 4:20 PM ET, the company’s “Mechazilla” launch tower successfully snagged the Starship vehicle using its massive metal arms for the first time.

We’ve seen these giant chopsticks catch the Super Heavy booster before, but this was the debut of the actual spacecraft surviving the mechanical embrace. It looked surprisingly graceful for a massive steel silo plummeting through the clouds.

Of course, it wouldn’t be a SpaceX flight without a little bit of "unplanned disassembly." While the Starship itself was saved, the booster flight ended with a literal bang when it aborted its landing and detonated over the Gulf of Mexico.

- Starship successfully performed its first-ever "chopstick" catch by the Mechazilla tower.
- The Super Heavy booster failed to return home, ending its journey in a fireball.
- The mission marks a significant step toward Musk's dream of fully reusable orbital hardware.

Elon Musk predicted success was "more likely than not" during the last earnings call, and he was mostly right. Even with the booster taking an unexpected swim, the data gathered from this catch is worth its weight in rocket fuel.

SpaceX is now investigating why the booster opted for a Viking funeral instead of a landing. Still, catching a spacecraft with oversized tongs is a hell of a way to spend an afternoon.
