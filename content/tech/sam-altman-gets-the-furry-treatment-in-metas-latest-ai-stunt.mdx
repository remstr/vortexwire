Meta’s AI lead Alexandr Wang just upped the ante in the Silicon Valley rivalry by posting a deepfake of OpenAI’s Sam Altman dressed as a furry mascot named Jolly. The clip features Altman, Mark Zuckerberg, and Wang on a mock video call, signaling that the future of tech marketing is apparently a psychedelic fever dream.

This stunt is part of a campaign for Muse, Meta’s consumer AI agent. Wang has ditched traditional PR for a strategy he calls "anti-PR," frequently roasting "corposlop" marketers while flooding Instagram with AI-generated chaos.

- The video passes Meta’s smell test because it includes a required AI label, keeping it within the company’s official policies.
- Conversely, static AI images aren't held to the same standard, which is how realistic fakes of actress Mia Goth and Scarlett Johansson continue to circulate without warnings.

While Meta insists its rules protect public figures, the line between parody and protection looks increasingly thin. If you’re a tech titan, you aren't just fighting for market share anymore; you’re fighting to keep your face off a digital furry suit.

The AI era hasn't just arrived; it’s making fun of your outfit. It’s a bold move for a platform that still struggles to tell the difference between a harmless joke and a deepfaked violation of likeness.
