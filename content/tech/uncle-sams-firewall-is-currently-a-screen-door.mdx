The federal government’s cybersecurity strategy currently resembles a screen door on a submarine. The FBI and USDA are the latest agencies to join a breach list that is officially getting longer than a CVS receipt.

The FBI’s National Crime Information Center (NCIC) was compromised, marking the agency's second major digital black eye in as many months. This isn't just low-level administrative fluff; we're talking about the primary database law enforcement uses for warrants and missing persons.

- The USDA breach started in a payments database but could act as a backdoor to sensitive food safety and animal-borne illness research.
- Previous hacks this fall already leaked records for almost every FBI employee and 40,000 DHS staffers.
- National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross has exited the chat, stepping down as the digital house burns.

President Trump confirmed the FBI hit on Fox News, sounding more like a curious spectator than a concerned commander-in-chief. He suggested the breach might actually be useful if it unearths evidence of voter fraud or those pesky "Russia, Russia, Russia" files.

This digital crime spree follows a week of massive "technical outages" at the FAA and NSA that the government insists were definitely not cyberattacks. At this rate, we’re about three weeks away from a teenager running the National Parks Service from a stolen iPad.
