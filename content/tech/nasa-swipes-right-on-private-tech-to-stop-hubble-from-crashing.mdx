Gravity is a relentless roommate that never pays rent and eventually kicks you out. After three decades of providing the most iconic screensavers in human history, the Hubble Space Telescope is being dragged slowly but surely back toward Earth’s atmosphere.

Without a literal pick-me-up, the legendary observatory is scheduled for a fiery, unceremonious retirement sometime in the mid-2030s. NASA isn’t ready to let the lights go out, but they are looking to the private sector to play hero.

The agency is essentially putting out a cosmic Bat-Signal for commercial partners. They’ve opened a Request for Information to see if private industry can provide a cost-effective way to boost Hubble’s orbit and keep the lenses open.

- The Job: Re-boost the telescope into a higher, more stable orbit to avoid a premature atmospheric funeral.
- The Deadline: Innovative firms have until November 30 at 3 PM ET to submit their "Save Hubble" business plans.
- The Stakes: Extending the life of a multi-billion-dollar asset for a fraction of the cost of a new launch.

Jared Isaacman notes that the agency owes it to taxpayers to squeeze every last drop of discovery out of this orbiting legend. If a commercial partner can pull off this high-altitude valet service, Hubble might just keep peering into the void for another decade.

It’s a gutsy pivot that turns a potential orbital disaster into a private-sector audition. Hopefully, someone out there has a very long tow rope and a bit of spare fuel.
