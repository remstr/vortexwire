If you've noticed a collective online eye-roll whenever AI pops up, you’re not alone. Instagram head Adam Mosseri recently spilled the digital tea, admitting that a lot of people just aren't vibing with artificial intelligence, and he predicts that negativity is "going to get worse."

Chatting on the *Colin and Samir* podcast, Mosseri wasn't just talking about Gen Z or the creative types who worry about job displacement. He acknowledged a broader, growing distaste for AI that extends well beyond the usual suspects. Think less "future is here!" and more "can we please just stop?"

This sentiment isn't merely academic; it’s playing out in public. Mosseri's comments came amidst discussions about a trend of college students booing tech CEOs at graduation ceremonies. It seems even cap-and-gown season isn't immune to the AI backlash.

So, what’s driving this widening chasm between tech optimism and public skepticism?
-   **Job Fear:** Creators and many professionals see AI as a direct threat.
-   **Ethical Quandaries:** Bias, misinformation, and autonomy concerns are paramount.
-   **General Distrust:** A feeling that AI is being rushed, often at users' expense.

Mosseri's take suggests this isn't a passing phase. Rather than hitting peak AI backlash, we might just be getting started, with public sentiment potentially growing more hostile as AI tools become ubiquitous. For those hoping for a calmer conversation around AI's integration into daily life, Mosseri’s forecast is decidedly gloomy, implying tech leaders better brace for more critical reception.
