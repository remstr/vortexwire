Kagi, the company that successfully convinced a niche group of nerds to pay for search results, is now coming for your inbox. Kagi Mail has officially entered public beta, positioning itself as the high-end, anti-Gmail for anyone tired of having their personal data harvested for "personalized" sneaker ads.

The pitch is aggressively straightforward: no surveillance, no ads, and no creepy "lock-in" tactics. For $99 a year—or $25 a month if you’re a Kagi Ultimate subscriber—you get a clean @kagimail.com address and 50GB of storage to house your digital clutter.

- **Privacy Heavyweight:** Built on open standards with zero data mining or tracking.
- **Customized:** Supports unlimited aliases and custom domains for the truly organized.
- **The Road Ahead:** Native mobile apps aren't slated to arrive until Q3 2027.

Kagi’s founders openly wondered if the world really needs another email provider. They decided the answer was yes, but only if that provider treats you like a human customer rather than a data point.

The service offers a 60-day free trial, giving you two months to figure out if you can survive on a mobile browser interface until the actual app drops in two years. It’s a bold move for the "technology should get out of your way" crowd, but if you’re already paying for their search engine, this is the logical next step in your messy Google divorce.
