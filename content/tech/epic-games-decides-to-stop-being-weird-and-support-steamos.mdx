Tim Sweeney’s long-running cold war with Valve just hit a surprising thaw. Epic Games has confirmed that its new Linux launcher is officially adding SteamOS to its guest list, alongside other distros like Fedora and Ubuntu.

Communications Director Jake Jones broke the news, clarifying that the launcher—currently in its preview phase—aims to be as platform-agnostic as possible. While the AppImage format is slated for a comeback later this month, the real story is Epic finally playing nice with the Steam Deck’s native environment.

- Epic’s Linux launcher is officially adding support for SteamOS, Fedora, Cachy, and Omarchy.
- AppImage packaging returns this month to keep the Linux purists happy and the downloads flexible.
- The "Fortnite on Deck" dream is still stuck in the lobby due to ongoing anti-cheat hurdles.

Before you start practicing your 90s on the bus, there’s a massive, anti-cheat-shaped asterisk to consider. Epic admits that Fortnite still isn't quite ready for a Steam Deck debut because of "EAC v2" requirements that the handheld doesn't currently meet.

Epic claims they are "actively talking" to Valve to bridge this technical rift, but for now, the launcher is just a gateway to the rest of your library. It’s a major step toward peace in our time, even if the Battle Bus is still circling the airfield indefinitely.
