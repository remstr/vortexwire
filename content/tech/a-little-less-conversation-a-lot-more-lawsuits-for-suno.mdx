Suno is finding out the hard way that mimicking legends isn’t just a party trick—it’s a massive legal liability. The estate of Elvis Presley and the publisher behind Eminem’s catalog are the latest heavy hitters to sue the AI music generator for allegedly turning human creativity into a "conveyor belt of cold hard cash."

The King’s team is leaning heavily on Tennessee’s ELVIS Act, a new law specifically designed to stop AI from snatching a singer’s likeness. Meanwhile, Eminem’s camp is tired of seeing the AI "mechanically encode" the hard-won identity of the Real Slim Shady to churn out cheap imitations.

- Elvis’s estate claims Suno violated voice-cloning protections.
- Eminem’s publisher alleges the platform is a "ravenous race to the bottom" for the arts.
- Suno argues its tech is for original creation and blocks specific artist prompts.

Lawyers for the plaintiffs aren’t mincing words, describing Suno’s business model as a cold replication of skills that take actual humans a lifetime to struggle with and perfect. It’s a spicy addition to Suno’s growing collection of legal headaches, which already includes lawsuits from Sony, Universal Music Group, and Reservoir Media.

Suno maintains it has guardrails to prevent misuse, but those walls look a bit thin when the King of Rock 'n' Roll is knocking them down. If these suits stick, the future of AI music might sound a lot more like a silent room than a Top 40 hit.
