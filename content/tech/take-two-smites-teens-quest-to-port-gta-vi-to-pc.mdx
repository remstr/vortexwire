Take-Two’s legal team isn’t known for its sense of adventure, especially when a 15-year-old tries to beat them to their own release date. This week, the publisher nuked "Leonida," a fan project dedicated to porting a leaked Xbox build of Grand Theft Auto VI to Windows.

The project, led by a teenager named NotRexed, vanished from GitHub after a DMCA takedown hit the main repository and nearly 2,900 forks. It turns out that translating raw Xbox code into a native PC experience is exactly the kind of "facilitating copyright infringement" that gets Rockstar’s lawyers out of bed in the morning.

- The project utilized a massive leak from "Cyberleek," who allegedly posted a full Xbox build of the game earlier this week.
- Modders are using "recompilation" to reverse-engineer the code, effectively rebuilding the game for PC from the ground up.
- Other underground efforts, like the "VIctory" project and the XCL compatibility layer, are still trying to cross the finish line despite the legal heat.

While modders are leveraging AI tools like Claude to speed up the reverse-engineering process, Take-Two is playing a high-stakes game of digital Whac-A-Mole. With rumors swirling that an official PC version might be closer than we think, it's a dead heat between Rockstar’s marketing department and a global community of code-monkeys. Take-Two might be winning the legal battle for now, but the internet has a funny way of making "impossible" ports happen anyway.
