Kevin Lewandowski founded Discogs twenty-five years ago, effectively turning the chaotic world of vinyl collecting into a searchable, obsessively detailed reality. Since then, the site has evolved into a mandatory destination for people who spend their weekends arguing over the sonic merits of obscure 1989 Ministry pressings.

While he spent decades organizing the world’s music database, his personal digital life is significantly more cluttered. Lewandowski currently juggles roughly 80 open browser tabs across his work and personal computers, a relatable nightmare for anyone prone to a late-night research spiral.

When he isn't enabling our collective physical media addiction, the man behind the marketplace is fueled by a surprisingly simple caffeine routine.

- **The Daily Driver:** His AeroPress. He uses the plastic plunger multiple times a day and refuses to live without a fresh cup in hand.
- **The Professional Lurker:** He scrolls through X and Instagram occasionally but treats posting like a social obligation he’d rather skip.
- **Smart Home Tinkering:** Instead of buying overpriced gadgets, he prefers building his own setups using Home Assistant to rig his garage doors and outdoor lights.
- **The One That Got Away:** He’s still chasing the vibe of Rez, the 2001 rhythm game that remains the gold standard for interactive, playable DJing.

It turns out the architect of the world’s most comprehensive music archive is just like the rest of us: over-caffeinated, digitally overextended, and forever obsessed with the perfect interface.
