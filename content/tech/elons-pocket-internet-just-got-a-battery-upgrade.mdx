Elon Musk is making "unplugging" a literal concept for the van-life crowd. The newly announced Starlink Mini 2 is a laptop-sized terminal that finally ditches the power cord, integrating the dish, Wi-Fi router, and a battery into one sleek, portable slab.

You can now pull high-speed satellite internet from the ether for about 4.5 hours on a single charge. If your wilderness retreat lasts longer than a Director’s Cut movie marathon, you can hot-swap extra batteries or plug into any 60W USB-C source.

The technical highlights:
- It’s remarkably slim at 23.1mm, though the integrated battery brings the total weight up to 3.5 pounds.
- It retains its IP67 dust and waterproof rating, ensuring it survives both desert dust and sudden mountain downpours.
- The $999 price tag includes a full year of Roam Unlimited service—a $2,100 value that makes the hardware feel like a steal.

Currently, SpaceX is keeping the guest list tight. Preorders are open by invitation only for select US customers, though a wider rollout is allegedly "coming soon."

It’s the ultimate flex for digital nomads who want to join a Zoom call from a canyon without hunting for a generator. It might be the first time "satellite internet" and "convenience" have appeared in the same sentence without a punchline. Just try not to spend a thousand bucks just to doomscroll in a tent.
