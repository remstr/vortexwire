Your smart TV box has a side hustle, and it’s not exactly legal. Google just confirmed that Videostrong, one of its Android manufacturing partners, spent years secretly turning 1.3 million devices into a massive, profit-seeking botnet.

This wasn't some accidental security flaw; it was an intentional, pre-installed backdoor. While you were binging prestige dramas, your set-top box was busy committing ad fraud and selling your network bandwidth to the highest-bidding cybercriminals.

The malware was baked directly into the hardware and triggered the moment users booted up for the first time. It turns out the digital underworld isn't just coming for your laptop—they want your karaoke machines, digital signage, and even your telehealth devices, too.

The fallout from the investigation includes:
- Google has "cut ties" and banned Videostrong from ever selling certified Android devices again.
- The botnet facilitated massive ad fraud, data exfiltration, and the creation of shady residential proxy networks.
- Dismantling the command-and-control domains likely severed access to an additional 10 million compromised devices worldwide.

Google claims it has overhauled its review process to prevent this kind of "inside job" from happening again. Still, it’s a grim reminder that if you buy a bargain-bin Android box from a brand you’ve never heard of, you might be inviting a tiny digital mobster into your living room. Check your hardware, or your karaoke machine might start racketeering.
