Jensen Huang, the man who turned black leather jackets into a trillion-dollar uniform, is finding himself in the hottest seat in Silicon Valley. Nvidia’s board just launched an independent investigation into their founder’s romantic ties with Alice Huang, an executive at a firm called Megaspeed.

The problem? U.S. intelligence officials suspect Alice wasn’t just in it for the tech talk—they believe she was a recruit for China’s Ministry of State Security. While the board claims they have "no reason to believe" national security was compromised, they’re hiring outside counsel to ensure the company’s crown jewels didn't end up in Beijing.

- Megaspeed is already under the Commerce Department’s microscope for allegedly smuggling high-end AI chips to China.
- Jensen admitted the relationship was romantic in a company memo after a New York Times report blew the whistle.
- There’s also the "Trump factor," with officials worried Jensen’s cozy access to the administration’s AI deliberations might have leaked.

The U.S. treats these AI chips like the modern-day Manhattan Project, so a potential honeytrap—intentional or not—is the ultimate corporate nightmare. Both the Biden and Trump administrations have kept a tight lid on chip exports to maintain technological supremacy over China.

Jensen says he’s fully cooperating and the board still has "confidence" in his leadership, but the vibe in the boardroom is likely anything but relaxed. It turns out that when you’re the gatekeeper of global compute, your dating life becomes a matter of national security.
