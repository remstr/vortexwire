The Binding of Isaac: Repentance Plus just got a crash course in the "find out" phase of generative AI. After Reddit sleuths discovered that a GTA-themed parody section featured music generated by Suno, Steam reviewers did what they do best: they scorched the game’s rating down to "Overwhelmingly Negative."

Creator Edmund McMillen, who has publicly trashed AI as "uninspired and bland," claims he was totally blindsided by the inclusion. It is a classic case of the creative lead not knowing what the production pipeline actually looks like, leaving studio Nicalis to scramble for the delete button.

- Redditors found Suno metadata buried in the game's audio files, leaving a digital paper trail straight to the AI platform.
- Nicalis updated the game within hours to scrub the robotic tunes, but the Steam rating is still stuck in purgatory.
- Valve’s strict disclosure rules require developers to fess up about using AI tools—a memo Nicalis seemingly ignored.

While fans are generally giving McMillen a pass due to his past anti-AI stance, the incident highlights the growing toxicity surrounding non-human assets in indie spaces. If you’re going to parody a billion-dollar franchise, maybe hire a human to write the jingle instead of asking a server farm to do the heavy lifting.

For now, the lesson is clear: in the world of indie gaming, authenticity is the only currency that actually matters. No one likes finding out their favorite dungeon crawler has been supplemented by an algorithm.
