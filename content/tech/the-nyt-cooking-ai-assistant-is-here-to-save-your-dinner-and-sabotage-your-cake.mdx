NYT Cooking has finally unleashed an AI assistant on its iOS app, and it’s basically a speed-reader for those thousands of reader comments you’re too tired to scroll through at 7 p.m. It’s designed to help subscribers scale recipes, find swaps for "bougie" ingredients, and handle the "chicken again?" dinner dread.

The tool was trained on the Times’ massive recipe archive and user feedback, meaning it knows that if you're out of makrut lime leaves, you need a realistic fix. Unlike chaotic chatbots that might suggest glue to keep your pizza cheese in place, this one stays grounded and refuses to entertain unhinged culinary requests.

However, don't fire your common sense just yet. During testing, the bot almost ruined a cake by suggesting exactly half the required butter—proving that while it’s great at logic, it still struggles with the "sugar and fat" soul of baking.

- It creates a new, customized recipe copy rather than overwriting the original, tested masterpiece.
- It’s a massive time-saver for anyone who finds the NYT comments section a bit too overwhelming during a weeknight rush.
- It still needs a babysitter for precise measurements, or you’ll end up with a very sad, dry Bundt.

This is a solid win for weeknight chicken triage, but it’s not quite ready for the Great British Bake Off. Keep an eye on the butter before you preheat the oven.
