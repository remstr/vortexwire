Apple has spent the last decade acting like the straight-A student who refuses to color outside the lines. Lately, however, the vibes in Cupertino have shifted from "meticulous refinement" to "let’s see what happens if we glue a screen to this."

The current obsession involves slapping touchscreens on MacBooks—a move Steve Jobs once famously derided as "ergonomically terrible." Now, we’re staring down a Pro line that might finally bridge the gap between a laptop and a giant iPad, whether we asked for it or not.

It’s messy, it’s arguably redundant, and it’s the most fun we’ve had watching the tech giant in years. Even the new HomeView is getting in on the action, ported over to run CarPlay apps in your living room because, apparently, we haven't reached peak screen saturation yet.

- Apple is officially embracing the "throw it at the wall" strategy with its latest hardware refreshes.
- The touchscreen MacBook Pro is finally a reality, even if it creates a massive identity crisis for the iPad Pro.
- Instagram’s Adam Mosseri is pivoting to long-form content while desperately trying to keep the platform from being buried by AI-generated sludge.

Meanwhile, Instagram is having a full-blown mid-life crisis. Mosseri is attempting to pivot the platform toward long-form video while navigating an increasingly toxic relationship with AI content that threatens to break the app's soul.

Between Apple's hardware experiments and social media's identity shifts, tech is currently a beautiful disaster. We'll take "weird" over "boring" every single time.
