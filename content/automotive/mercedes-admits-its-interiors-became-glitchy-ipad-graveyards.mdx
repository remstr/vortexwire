It turns out that trying to adjust the AC through three sub-menus while going 70 mph isn’t "innovation"—it’s a liability. Mercedes-Benz, the brand that once thought slapping a slab of glass across the entire dashboard was the future, is finally admitting they over-indexed on the iPad aesthetic.

During a recent Reddit Q&A, CEO Ola Källenius confessed that while the digital pivot followed industry trends, some things just belong in the physical world. He even admitted to a personal fondness for "a good rocker and roller" on a steering wheel, a sentiment shared by anyone who has ever wrestled with a haptic slider.

- The "Hyper-Digital" era is cooling off as drivers demand tactile feedback for core functions.
- Models like the GLS, GLE, and S-Class are leading the charge back to physical switches.
- Digital screens aren't dying, but they’re being relegated to tasks that don't require muscle memory.

Don't expect a full-blown return to the 90s. Källenius insists that "simply turning back the clock" isn't the move, but finding a balance between tech flexibility and old-school reliability is the new goal.

Mercedes is essentially acknowledging that while we love big screens for navigation, we’d rather not use a glass pane to defrost the windows. It’s a rare moment of corporate humility suggesting the "button-less" trend has finally hit the brakes.
