Most people struggle to commit to a 30-minute gym session, but Arizona’s Fat Fender Garage just spent 15,000 hours—roughly 1.7 years of non-stop labor—perfecting a single truck. Unveiled at SEMA, the "GT/FX" is a 1956 Ford F-100 that stopped being a farmhand and started identifying as a supercar.

Under the "pancaked" hood sits a third-gen 5.0-liter Coyote V8 topped with a 3.0-liter Whipple supercharger. This setup screams out 800 horsepower and 750 lb-ft of torque, essentially making it a Mustang GT in a vintage steel trench coat.

The fabrication work is genuinely obsessive, with every panel massaged to perfection. From the widened fenders to the grafted-on Shelby GT350 rear diffuser, it’s a masterclass in aggressive metalwork that makes standard restomods look lazy.

- 800 HP Coyote V8 paired with a 10-speed automatic and Borla XR-1 exhaust.
- FF1 Performance Coilover Chassis engineered for track-day cornering, not hauling gravel.
- Full custom leather interior featuring a "hidden" climate control system and modern audio.

The only part of the exterior they didn't touch was the original grille. Everything else is a high-dollar flex finished in custom BASF paint and Cerakote trim, proving some things are actually worth the wait—and the massive invoice. It’s a 1.7-year labor of love that hauls much more than just plywood.
