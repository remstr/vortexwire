Elon Musk finally released POV footage of the Tesla Roadster’s "flying" stunt, and it looks exactly like something a kid would do with a physics-defying cheat code in GTA. The uncrewed prototype flipped and drove upside down, fueled by a cold-gas thruster system that packs 10,000 psi of high-pressure nitrogen right behind the driver’s head.

It’s undeniably impressive tech, but the logistical reality is currently a bit of a nightmare. That 200-liter tank provides just a few seconds of "flight" but requires up to six hours to refill—though Tesla pinky-promises they’ll eventually get that downtime under 30 minutes.

- The "Rocket" trim is a single-seat kickflip machine that likely won't ever see a public road.
- Production is tentatively slated for 2028, which translates from "Tesla Time" to "maybe by the end of the decade."
- The base model skips the thrusters but still claims a sub-two-second 0-60 launch.

While Ferrari’s CEO is busy praising the dream, the actual road-going reality is a lot more grounded. A car that shoots high-pressure gas out of its sides and bottom isn't exactly neighborhood-friendly or street-legal.

For now, the Roadster remains a very expensive, very fast science project. It’s perfect for generating TikTok clout and keeping the hype train fueled, but you might want to hold off on trading in your Corolla for a flying EV just yet.
