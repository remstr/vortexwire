The Polestar 2 is officially dead. After a 200,000-unit run, the Chinese assembly line has reportedly spat out its final electric fastback.

Born from a Volvo concept that pivoted to chase Tesla’s ballooning stock price, the "2" was the car that actually made Polestar a standalone brand. Now, it’s making way for a 2027 refresh and a future "7" SUV—though US drivers shouldn't hold their breath.

Thanks to a frosty reception from the Commerce Department over connected vehicle rules, Polestar is essentially ghosting the American market. But before they hand back the keys, they’re throwing a massive "everything must go" fire sale.

- Production of the Polestar 2 has officially ended after a successful four-year run.
- Regulatory hurdles are forcing the brand to shift its focus away from the US.
- Dealers are offering massive rebates up to $25,000 on remaining Polestar 3 and 4 inventory.

The Polestar 3 SUV might claim a $67,500 sticker price, but that’s just a suggestion at this point. Dealers are slashing up to $23,000 off the 3 and a whopping $25,000 off the 4.

It’s a weirdly abrupt ending for a brand that tried to blend Swedish safety with Chinese tech. If you’ve ever wanted a minimalist 500-horsepower EV for a bargain-basement price, now is the time to strike before Polestar becomes a "remember them?" trivia question.
