After a hiatus that felt longer than the wait for a table at Hell’s Kitchen, Top Gear is officially coming back from the dead. BBC Studios confirmed that culinary firebrand Gordon Ramsay will headline the show’s newest iteration, scheduled to hit screens in 2028.

The series has been "resting" since 2023, following a horrific high-speed crash that left former host Andrew Flintoff with life-altering injuries. Now, the network is betting that Ramsay’s signature brand of high-octane aggression is exactly what’s needed to jumpstart the flailing franchise.

- Filming kicks off next year, giving Ramsay plenty of time to practice yelling at slow-moving traffic.
- Comedian Jimmy Carr is rumored to be in the mix for a co-hosting spot, though the BBC is keeping the final trio under wraps for now.
- This marks the show's fourth major reboot since the legendary Clarkson, Hammond, and May era ended in a flurry of steak-related drama nearly a decade ago.

Ever since the original trio defected to Amazon, the BBC has struggled to find a lineup with genuine chemistry. We’ve seen everyone from Matt LeBlanc to Chris Evans try to steer the ship, usually with all the charisma of a rental car agent.

Ramsay, a noted petrolhead with a massive private garage, says he’s bringing "fresh energy" to the project. Let’s just hope he treats a malfunctioning gearbox with more patience than he treats a raw scallop.
