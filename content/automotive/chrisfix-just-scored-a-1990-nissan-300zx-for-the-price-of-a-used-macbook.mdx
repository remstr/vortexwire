Most people find spiders and regret in old barns. YouTuber ChrisFix found a yellow 1990 Nissan 300ZX that hasn’t seen a highway since "The Boy Is Mine" topped the Billboard charts.

This T-top Z32 sat untouched for 28 years before the DIY guru talked the seller down from a $4,900 eBay listing to a cool $2,300. In a market where twin-turbo versions are fetching nearly $50,000, snagging a five-speed manual Z for two grand feels like car-culture grand larceny.

The specs are pure 90s nostalgia: a naturally aspirated 3.0-liter V6 pushing 222 horsepower when it left the factory. With 106,000 miles on the clock, it’s a relatively fresh specimen that simply fell asleep during the Clinton administration.

- The car hasn't been registered since 1998, effectively missing the entire "Fast & Furious" era.
- ChrisFix has already performed a cosmetic miracle, polishing faded paint and swapping a trashed bumper for a fresh one.
- Despite the showroom shine, the engine hasn't actually coughed to life yet.

While it looks ready for a Radwood meet, the mechanical reality is likely a horror show of ancient fuel and brittle rubber. Reviving a car that’s been stationary since the iMac G3 was high-tech requires more than just a fresh battery and hope. 

ChrisFix has the aesthetic side handled, but the real drama starts when he tries to wake up those dormant horses. If it runs, he’s officially won the "barn find" lottery for 2024.
