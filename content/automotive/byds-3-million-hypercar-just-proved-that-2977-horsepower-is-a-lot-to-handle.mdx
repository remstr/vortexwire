BYD’s Yangwang U9 Xtreme is designed to do two things: dance on its suspension and melt asphalt. During a recent high-speed run in China, it settled for a third option: disintegrating against a track barrier.

The 2,977-horsepower monster was prepping to shatter the 311-mph barrier when things went sideways—literally. Social media footage shows a wreck so violent it ripped the entire rear axle assembly away from the car, leaving the $3 million EV looking less like a hypercar and more like a very expensive Lego set.

- The Damage: Both ends of the car are trashed, but the carbon-fiber "Super Cabin" did its job.
- The Survivor: Despite the carnage, the test driver climbed out of the wreckage unassisted and uninjured.
- The Tech: No battery fires were reported, which is a massive win for BYD’s 1,200-volt architecture.

This particular U9 Xtreme is already the reigning king of production EVs, having clocked 308.3 mph back in September. It uses four independent motors to manage torque at each wheel, though even that level of electronic wizardry couldn't keep it on the tarmac this time.

BYD hasn't pinpointed the exact cause of the failure yet, but they aren't backing down. The plan is still to head to the UAE to finally crack that 500 km/h mark—assuming they can find enough spare parts to build a new one.
