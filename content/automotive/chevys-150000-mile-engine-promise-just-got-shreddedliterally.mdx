GM claims its 1.2-liter three-cylinder can go 150,000 miles before needing a timing belt swap. A recent teardown suggests that marketing claim belongs in the fiction section right next to honest Tinder profiles.

YouTuber Eric from "I Do Cars" recently dissected an LIH engine from a 2021 Trailblazer with only 84,000 miles on the clock. What he found wasn't just a breakdown; it was a mechanical horror show of "emulsified" rubber and pulverized metal.

The culprit is the "wet" timing belt, which is designed to live inside the engine's oiling system to reduce friction. Instead of lasting 15 years, it disintegrated prematurely, choking the oil pump with fibers and letting the pistons play a violent game of tag with the valves.

If you’re driving one of these "eco" machines, that 150,000-mile service interval starts to look less like a promise and more like a dare.
- Affected models include the Chevy Trailblazer, Trax, Envista, and the Buick Encore GX.
- The teardown revealed bent valves on every cylinder and deep gouges across both camshafts.
- Once those belt fibers clog the oil pickup, the engine effectively begins to eat itself from the inside out.

While this is just one engine, it serves as a grim warning for anyone trusting the owner's manual over common sense. If your powertrain warranty is nearing its 60,000-mile expiration, you might want to start listening very closely for any strange rhythmic thumping under the hood.
