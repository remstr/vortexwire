James May, the man who spent decades testing hypercars and getting lost in the Italian countryside, has finally found his automotive soulmate. It’s not a million-dollar exotic, but a tiny, $25,000 electric truck from a startup called Slate.

The "Captain Slow" seal of approval isn't easy to earn, especially for a vehicle that looks like it was designed by a minimalist architect on a budget. Yet, after taking the pint-sized pickup for a spin, May’s verdict was shockingly definitive: "I’d have one."

What’s the appeal of a truck that’s essentially a glorified golf cart with a bed? It’s the radical simplicity. In a world where modern trucks are turning into bloated, rolling computers, Slate is betting on a "back-to-basics" philosophy that values function over flash.

- The interior features manual windows and actual physical buttons—a revolutionary concept in 2024.
- There’s no massive infotainment screen; you just clip your phone into a mount and call it a day.
- The base model offers 150 miles of range, while a beefier battery can push that to 240.

It’s a refreshingly honest take on the EV market. While Detroit is busy building 9,000-pound behemoths meant to survive a lunar landing, Slate is making something that can actually navigate a grocery store parking lot.

If a guy who has driven every supercar on the planet is ready to go small, maybe we’ve finally reached peak "over-engineered garbage." Or maybe May just realizes that a $25,000 price tag is the only thing more refreshing than a cold gin and tonic.
