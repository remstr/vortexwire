NASCAR Cup Series driver Carson Hocevar just gave his local Homeowners Association a reason to double their blood pressure medication. What started as a friendly $800 "cheap car challenge" against buddy Vernon Hale ended with a decommissioned tank parked in a residential driveway.

Hocevar’s weapon of choice was a weary Chevy Cobalt that barely survived a botched rattle-can paint job before the mechanical failures piled up. After a steady diet of the wrong fuel and a catastrophic power-steering belt failure, the Cobalt gave up the ghost while being towed home by Hale’s Oldsmobile.

Rather than calling a scrap yard, the duo opted for a more permanent, albeit louder, solution. A surplus military tank arrived on a trailer, narrowly avoiding weigh-station drama and sidewalk-destroying turns before lining up its target.

- The Cobalt was flattened in a single pass, turning Hocevar's "fragile" investment into a very thin, metal pancake.
- An HOA representative on-site was notably unhappy about the armored guest or the potential threat to the neighborhood's septic system.
- Neighbors and passing truckers stopped to gawk at the absurdity of a tank-assisted demolition derby in a suburban yard.

It’s a bold strategy for property value management. While the Chevy is officially retired, Hocevar now has to explain to the board why there’s several tons of steel sitting precariously close to the property’s plumbing. Sometimes, the most efficient way to deal with a lemon is 50 tons of armored authority.
