The Ineos Grenadier looks like a classic Land Rover Defender that spent a summer at CrossFit, and soon, it’ll be as American as a Costco rotisserie chicken. British billionaire Jim Ratcliffe’s automotive passion project is officially moving its production line to the States in 2028.

It’s a logical pivot considering Americans currently snap up roughly 70 percent of all Grenadiers sold globally. If you’re selling that many old-school, body-on-frame SUVs to people in Montana and suburban Dallas, you might as well build them in their backyard.

- **The Lineup:** Both the flagship Grenadier SUV and the Quartermaster pickup are slated for domestic assembly.
- **The Strategy:** Ineos is currently scouting factory locations, with a final decision on the specific state expected in the coming months.
- **The Global Shuffle:** Their current French plant in Hambach might be sold to China’s BYD, signaling a massive shift in the brand’s manufacturing DNA.

CEO Lynn Calder is calling this the "biggest moment" in the company’s short history. It turns out that "pro-growth" U.S. policies and our insatiable appetite for boxy rigs that look good covered in mud are a winning combination.

While we don't know the exact investment figures yet, the move cements Ineos as more than just a niche European import. It’s a calculated bet that the future of rugged off-roading belongs to the American market.
