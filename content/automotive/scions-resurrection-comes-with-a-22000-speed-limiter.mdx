Toyota is officially bringing Scion back from the dead, but don’t go looking for a sporty tC revival. Instead, we’re getting the Scion Kei-01, a pint-sized utility truck that moves with all the grace and velocity of a hungover sloth.

Toyota USA’s engineering VP, Don Federico, suggested $22,000 is a "reasonable" price for this miniature hauler. For context, that’s just a few grand shy of a Ford Maverick—a vehicle that can actually handle a highway without being trampled by a soccer mom in an Odyssey.

- The Specs: A 660-cc engine, CVT, and a strictly enforced 25-mph top speed to skirt federal safety regs.
- The Utility: A fold-down bed with a 660-pound capacity, perfect for hauling hay or your shattered dreams of reaching the speed limit.
- The Quirks: It’s right-hand drive only, so prepare to master the art of the reverse drive-thru maneuver.

This isn't a Silverado killer; it's a glorified golf cart designed for farm work and university campuses. While the cabin offers "creature comforts" like power windows and a touchscreen, it lacks the most important feature of a modern vehicle: the ability to outpace a brisk jogger.

Production starts in Indiana in late 2027. Toyota is targeting fleet buyers first, but if the demand for overpriced, slow-motion pickups is high enough, they might let the general public join the crawl.
