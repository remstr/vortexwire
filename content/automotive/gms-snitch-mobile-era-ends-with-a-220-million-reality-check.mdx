It turns out your Chevy wasn’t just a ride; it was a high-speed informant. General Motors just agreed to fork over $220 million to 36 states and D.C. after getting caught selling your driving data to insurance companies like a tech-savvy Judas.

From 2014 to 2024, GM’s "Smart Driver" program vacuumed up details on how hard you brake and how often you treat the interstate like a NASCAR qualifying lap. While you were cruising, GM was whispering your secrets to brokers, who then gave insurers the green light to hike your premiums.

- Dealership staff allegedly signed people up without consent to bag "signup bonuses" from the automaker.
- The OnStar app reportedly told 370,000 drivers they weren’t enrolled while their data continued to flow to brokers.
- Information sold included specific timestamps of your speeding habits and exact travel times.

The automaker killed the program in April 2024 and has since been ghosted by data brokers like LexisNexis and Verisk. Beyond the payout, the FTC has officially banned GM from selling sensitive driver data, forcing the company to adopt a legitimate privacy program.

Moving forward, GM must get your "affirmative express consent" before they can monetize your lead foot. It’s a costly lesson in the fact that your car’s computer shouldn't be moonlighting as an insurance adjuster.
