Rob Dahm is the patron saint of rotary-induced madness, and his latest SEMA build is a masterclass in mechanical gaslighting. He managed to stuff 13 rotors into a first-gen Mazda Miata, a feat that defies both physics and financial common sense.

The catch? It’s a glorious, high-stakes prank. A massive 12-rotor engine sits prominently in the bay, looking fully functional with its complex fuel lines and wiring, but it’s purely for the "wow" factor.

- A hidden single-rotor engine with a Garrett turbo actually handles the propulsion.
- The 12-rotor display piece claims a theoretical, soul-crushing 4,500 horsepower.
- Four turbochargers total are squeezed into the bay, adding immense weight to the front end.

Dahm’s team had to fabricate custom, triangulated mounts just to stop the chassis from buckling under the engine's "teeter-totter" weight distribution. Loading it into a trailer for the Las Vegas show was its own logistical nightmare, involving steep ramps and a very real risk of pancaking the undercarriage.

It’s the ultimate "because I can" project for the Haltech booth. While the 12-rotor assembly is essentially the world’s most impressive ornament, the car does move under its own power thanks to its secret heart.

It’s loud, it’s unnecessary, and it’s exactly why SEMA exists. If you want to see a Miata that’s 90% engine and 10% audacity, Dahm has delivered.
