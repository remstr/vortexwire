McLaren just left a giant orange bird in London to tell us their next supercar is going to be an absolute menace. A "Speedy Kiwi" statue—a nod to founder Bruce McLaren’s New Zealand roots—popped up in Covent Garden sporting the "McL 820" moniker on its flank.

If you know how Woking operates, those numbers aren't just for show. The brand’s naming convention historically tracks metric horsepower, meaning the 820 translates to a spicy 809 bhp. For those keeping score, that's a massive leap over the outgoing 750S.

- The 750S is scheduled to retire in 2027, leaving a supercar-shaped hole this hybrid beast will fill.
- McLaren is finally ditching outside engine suppliers to build its own powertrains in-house at a brand-new UK facility.

The "McL" prefix marks a clean break from the old naming scheme, following the lead of the manual-gearbox McL 6GT we saw at Monterey. It’s a rebrand that trades the clinical, alphanumeric feel of the past for something slightly more visceral.

While the company hasn’t released a formal PR blast yet, the writing is literally on the statue. Between the new V8 hybrid powertrains and looming rumors of a future SUV, McLaren is aggressively signaling that its next era won't just be electrified—it’ll be significantly meaner. Expect the 820 to be the new benchmark for the mainstream lineup.
