Forget the eulogies for the V8. Rob Dahm just proved the new 2026 Dodge Charger "Sixpack" is a savage in its own right, provided you’re willing to make the transmission cry for its mother.

Dahm took the 3.0-liter twin-turbo inline-six—affectionately known as the Hurricane—and performed a surgical strike. Without cracking open the engine or touching a single internal component, he swapped the twin-turbos for one massive single unit, cranked the boost to a terrifying 46 psi, and fed it a steady diet of E85 and nitrous.

The result? A wheel-spinning 968 horsepower and 863 lb-ft of torque. For those keeping score at home, that’s nearly double the stock high-output rating of 550 hp.

- Stock internals: The engine block and rotating assembly didn't explode, which is a miracle of modern engineering.
- Power jump: We’re looking at a 400+ hp increase over the factory spec without a single forged piston in sight.
- The weak link: The eight-speed transmission started shifting like it was having a mid-life crisis once the torque hit.

While the gearbox didn’t technically grenade, it clearly wasn’t a fan of the four-figure power party. It began throwing a tantrum to save its own skin, preventing the car from hitting that elusive 1,000-hp milestone on the dyno. 

Still, the fact that the Hurricane can handle this much pressure suggests Mopar fans will be just fine in the post-Hemi era. Just maybe start saving for a heavy-duty transmission swap now.
