Chevrolet famously ditched the manual transmission for the C8 Corvette, claiming the "market voted with its wallet" for automatics. That’s corporate-speak for "we didn't want to spend the R&D cash on a mid-engine stick shift."

Enter Peitz Performance Tune, the aftermarket heroes currently doing God’s work for the three-pedal purists. They’ve successfully engineered a way to cram a Tremec manual into the C8, but satisfying your left foot comes with a staggering financial reality check.

- The conversion will set you back between $60,000 and $70,000—and that’s before you buy the actual car.
- It’s currently only compatible with the base Stingray, so Z06 owners are out of luck for now.
- Don't clear your garage yet; full production isn’t slated to begin until mid-2027.

To make this Frankenstein's monster breathe, Peitz performed digital surgery using a MoTeC control unit to bypass Chevy’s notoriously locked-down software. The interior gets a sleek makeover as well, replacing those divisive push-button gear selectors with a proper, honest-to-god shifter.

The new custom console manages to keep the iconic "Wall of Buttons" intact while housing the mechanical hardware. It’s a labor-intensive process that requires significantly more than just a wrench and a dream.

Is paying the price of a second Corvette just to row your own gears rational? Absolutely not.

But in a world of increasingly sanitized driving experiences, someone had to stand up for the gearheads—even if that stand costs more than a college degree.
