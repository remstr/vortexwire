The internet loves a good "EV batteries are glorified paperweights" horror story, but Ford just showed up with the receipts to ruin the narrative. After poking around the internals of 36,000 first-year Mustang Mach-Es, the Blue Oval discovered that these packs aren't just surviving—they’re thriving.

The 2021 cohort is currently sporting an average battery health of 92%. Even the road warriors with over 100,000 miles on the odometer are sitting pretty at 91% health, proving that the fear of a $20,000 replacement bill by year five is mostly just a fever dream.

- **The Long Game:** Over 30 "hyper-milers" have already cruised past the 200,000-mile mark while retaining a respectable 88% capacity.
- **Maintenance 101:** Ford suggests preconditioning while plugged in and parking in the shade to keep the NCM chemistry happy.
- **Charging Habits:** Stick to Level 2 chargers for the daily grind and save the DC fast charging for your actual road trips.

While newer models are pivoting toward even more robust Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) packs, these original units are holding their own. Multiple studies confirm the trend: modern EV batteries are less like a dying smartphone and more like a marathon runner who refuses to quit.

Unless you’re actively trying to bake your car in a desert parking lot, your Mach-E is going to be just fine. The only thing truly dying here is the argument that electric cars are disposable.
