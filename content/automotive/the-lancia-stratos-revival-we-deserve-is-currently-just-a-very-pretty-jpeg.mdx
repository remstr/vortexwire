Lancia hasn't quite recaptured its 1970s rally magic lately, so digital artist Luca Serafini decided to do the heavy lifting for them. His modern Stratos rendering is a punchy love letter to the original "wedge" that crushed the WRC between 1974 and 1976.

Most modern revivals feel like a bloated corporate committee designed them, but Serafini kept the stubby wheelbase and wraparound glass that made the original look like a low-flying spaceship. Best of all? He kept the pop-up headlights—a move that deserves a Nobel Prize in automotive aesthetics.

The project highlights:
- Widebody fenders and a rear spoiler big enough to serve a four-course dinner on.
- Two distinct flavors: a "road" version in classic Azzurro Chiaro and a track monster rocking the legendary Marlboro livery.
- Based on Marcello Gandini’s original DNA but pushed to "extreme" modern proportions.

Serafini didn't set out to build a pixel-perfect replica. Instead, he took the core volumes that made the Stratos a legend and sharpened the surfacing to meet 2024 standards without losing the car's soul.

The artist is currently refining a final version with even more realistic detailing. Lancia, if you’re reading this, please stop staring at your crossover spreadsheets for five minutes and take some notes.
