Ford is heading back to the 24 Hours of Le Mans, and this time, they’ve decided to stop asking for help. The new GT41 hypercar is a 41-inch-tall love letter to the 1960s, powered by a 5.4-liter hybrid V8 that is being built entirely in-house.

Unlike Ford’s NASCAR or GT3 programs, which lean on outside experts, this engine is a pure Dearborn solo project. They aren’t just designing the heavy hardware; they’re coding the software and even wiring the wipers. 

- **The Name:** It stands exactly 41 inches tall, a literal inch taller than the legendary GT40.
- **The Power:** A naturally aspirated hybrid V8 that begins life as a standard Coyote production block.
- **The Ego:** Developed entirely by Ford Racing in a labyrinthine lab that’s been around since 1949.

Engineers are currently torturing these motors in a dyno cell just feet away from where the original Le Mans-winning engines were tested. During digital simulations of the Mulsanne Straight, the exhaust headers glow a demonic red while the V8 wails at full tilt.

Powertrain chief Christian Hertrich admits they’ll likely hit a few walls before they find the podium. It’s a refreshingly honest "DIY or die" attitude in an industry usually obsessed with sterile corporate PR.

If engine number four survives its simulated marathon, we’ll see if Ford’s internal grit can actually conquer France next year.
