Bentley’s engineers decided customers shouldn’t be the first to break their new electric SUV. So, they sent the Torcal, the automaker’s inaugural EV, on a global tour of pain, logging over 621,000 miles of extreme testing across the planet's harshest climates.

Think minus 36 degrees in Sweden, sweltering 104-degree desert heat, and thin air runs above 14,000 feet at Pikes Peak. This wasn't a joyride; it was a mission to confirm the 876-horsepower, 996 lb-ft torque beast is truly ready for prime time.

Under the hood – or rather, in the code – Bentley approached development more like a tech startup than a traditional luxury carmaker. Software updates arrived every six weeks, mirroring the rapid cycles you'd expect from your smartphone, not a coachbuilt vehicle.

The Torcal’s hefty 1,378-pound battery, an 800-volt, 109.0 kWh usable unit, isn't just about range; it's about toughness. It survived a 99 mph shot from a 30mm steel projectile and is designed to resist fire for two hours, proving this EV can take a hit. Fast charging from 10-80% takes just 16 minutes, keeping it competitive with other high-end EVs. Basically, Bentley wants you to know your future luxury electric SUV has been to hell and back so you can enjoy the ride.
