Mate Rimac just announced that the Nevera—the Croatian electric lightning bolt that made physics look like a suggestion—is reaching the end of the road. Despite an original plan to build 150 of these 1,914-horsepower monsters, the production line is stopping at just 71 units.

The final ten Nevera Rs will be delivered by the end of the year, marking the sunset of an era that was as quiet as it was terrifyingly fast. It turns out that in the stratosphere of the ultra-wealthy, selling a pure EV hypercar is a tougher lift than selling a hybrid with a soul.

- Final Tally: 51 standard Neveras and 20 track-focused Nevera Rs will exist in the wild.
- Brutal Specs: The Nevera R hits 60 mph in 1.74 seconds, which is roughly the time it takes to regret your lunch choices.
- Claim to Fame: It officially retires as the world's best-selling electric hypercar, a title that is both impressive and a reminder of how niche this market remains.

Mate Rimac isn’t crying over the production "shortfall," though. He’s framing the Nevera as the high-voltage sacrificial lamb that funded the company's massive growth and the new Bugatti Tourbillon. 

Essentially, the Nevera ran so that a V16 Bugatti could scream. It served its purpose as a rolling tech demo, proving that Rimac can build the future—even if the world’s billionaires prefer to keep one foot in the past.
