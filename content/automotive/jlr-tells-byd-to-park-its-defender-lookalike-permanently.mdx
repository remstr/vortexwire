Jaguar Land Rover just proved that "boxy" is a protected personality trait—at least when it looks exactly like a Defender. A Dutch court ruled that BYD’s Ti7 SUV is a bit too fond of JLR’s homework, banning the Chinese automaker from importing, selling, or even storing the vehicle anywhere in the European Union.

The Ti7 is a plug-in hybrid with the same upright windows and rear-mounted spare wheel we’ve seen on every rugged British trail for decades. Despite BYD’s lawyers arguing that the headlights and bumper layout were distinct, the judge wasn't buying the "it’s a different car" defense, citing the unmistakable Defender silhouette as the deciding factor.

- **The Fine Print:** BYD faces a staggering €100,000 daily fine if they violate the court's order.
- **The Strategy:** JLR relied on a 2018 design registration, securing their rights before the current-gen Defender even hit the showroom floor.

In a move of peak corporate sass, BYD told the court they had no "concrete plans" to launch the Ti7 in Europe anyway. It’s the automotive equivalent of getting banned from a club you claim you weren't trying to enter. 

Still, JLR isn’t interested in seeing their luxury-rugged aesthetic diluted by a budget doppelgänger. If BYD wants to conquer the EU, they’ll have to find a shape that doesn't trigger a massive sense of déjà vu for Land Rover owners.
