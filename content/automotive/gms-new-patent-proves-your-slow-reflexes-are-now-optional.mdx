General Motors just patented a way to take the "human" out of the human-machine interface. If US Patent 12,780,203 ever makes it to production, your next Corvette won’t need your twitchy right foot to win a drag race; it’ll just need a clear line of sight to the Christmas Tree.

The system uses onboard cameras to stare down the staging lights and calculate the exact millisecond to fire. It handles the braking, torque staging, and throttle, effectively turning a high-stakes quarter-mile sprint into a very expensive version of Guitar Hero.

- **The Hardware:** Onboard cameras and software replace the driver’s nervous system to nail the perfect launch without a pedal touch.
- **The Logic:** The AI distinguishes between "Pro" and "Sportsman" light sequences, so it knows exactly when the green is coming before it even flickers.
- **The Catch:** There is no word yet on how the tech handles rain, bad track lighting, or the inevitable salt from purists in the other lane.

While Road & Track notes this could usher in an era of "push-button drag racing," traditionalists are likely already typing up angry forum posts. GM hasn't committed to a production timeline, but the dream of winning a trophy while essentially being a passenger is officially on the books. It’s the ultimate "work smarter, not harder" play for the weekend warrior.
