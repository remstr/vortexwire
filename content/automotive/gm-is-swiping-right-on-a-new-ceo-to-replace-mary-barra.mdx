Mary Barra has been at General Motors since she was 18—a tenure longer than most marriages and almost every tech startup in existence. She broke the ultimate glass ceiling in 2014, but after a decade of steering the Detroit giant through bankruptcy recovery and a messy pivot to electric, she might finally be looking for an exit.

Reports suggest GM is working with an executive search firm to find its next leader. While Barra hasn't officially announced she's retiring, the "quiet search" is definitely getting loud in the C-suite.

Her legacy is a mixed bag of massive profits and expensive pivots. Under her watch, GM's stock price surged 136%, but her aggressive "all-in" bet on EVs has met a lukewarm market, and the multi-billion-dollar Cruise autonomous taxi dream ended in a high-profile shutdown.

The short list of potential drivers includes:
- Mark Reuss: The current president and ultimate internal lifer who’s been with the company since 1983.
- Paul Jacobson: The CFO who previously kept the books at Delta Air Lines.
- Sterling Anderson: The new Chief Product Officer with a resume that includes Tesla and Silicon Valley startups.

Barra is currently 64, and while she hasn't cleared out her desk just yet, the board is clearly ready for a fresh set of hands on the wheel. Turning around a legacy automaker is a brutal job; doing it twice might be asking too much of anyone.
