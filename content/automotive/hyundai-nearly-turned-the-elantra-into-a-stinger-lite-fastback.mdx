The Elantra’s "Art of Steel" look is already sharp, but it could have been a lot more adventurous. Hyundai designers initially pitched the new sedan as a sleek liftback, aiming for that "Stinger-lite" energy before reality set in.

Design chief Simon Loasby admits the team flirted with a fastback roofline and a massive glass hatch. Ultimately, they realized that aesthetics don't matter much if your rear passengers are forced to slouch like grumpy teenagers.

- Headroom is king: A sloping roof would have sacrificed the Elantra’s best-in-class rear space and added weight via heavy hinges and glass.
- The wagon that wasn't: A long-roof variant was also briefly on the table, but Hyundai eventually doubled down on the traditional sedan layout.
- Growth spurt: The final product is longer and wider than its predecessor, proving that interior volume beats a "cool" silhouette every time.

Under the hood, the base 2.0-liter puts out a modest 147 hp, though the upcoming 2028 hybrid is the real efficiency play. That model targets a massive 53 mpg, which is great for anyone who treats gas stations like a plague.

If you actually care about 0-60 times, the 276-hp Elantra N remains the high-performance darling of the lineup. Rumors suggest the next generation might even take styling cues from the gorgeous, retro-futuristic N Vision 74 concept.
