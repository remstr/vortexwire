Phil Collins is finally being inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame as a solo artist this month, proving that you can indeed find success if you try just a little bit harder. For decades, Collins was the easiest target in music—the ubiquitous face of 80s overexposure who somehow turned "sad divorced dad energy" into a global empire.

While critics spent years mocking his gated-reverb drums and polished ballads, the Rock Hall is finally acknowledging the sheer intensity behind the hits. It turns out that selling 150 million records isn't just luck; it’s the result of being the hardest-working man in pop who simply refused to leave the radio.

- He’s one of only three artists to sell over 100 million albums both as a solo act and as part of a group, joining Paul McCartney and Michael Jackson.
- That "In the Air Tonight" drum fill remains the undisputed heavyweight champion of air-drumming moments at suburban weddings.
- He successfully transitioned from a prog-rock drummer in Genesis to a Disney-soundtrack legend without losing his technical edge.

Collins famously joked that he knew he wasn't "cool," but he was certainly prolific. This induction serves as a long-overdue reminder that while he might have been the soundtrack to your dentist’s lobby, he was also a technical powerhouse. It’s time to stop apologizing for owning a copy of *No Jacket Required*.
