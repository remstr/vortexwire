Sarah Sze is currently pulling off the kind of multi-city residency that would make a touring rock star sweat. With new installations popping up in New Haven, Brooklyn, and San Francisco, she’s reminding us that contemporary art is more than just shiny things for billionaires to store in tax-free warehouses.

The standout here isn’t just the visual "constellations" Sze is known for—those intricate, dizzying webs of everyday objects that look like a junk drawer achieved enlightenment. This time, the real magic is happening in the ears.

- In San Francisco, Sze has unveiled a sculpted soundscape that’s doing more than just filling empty space with generic ambient hums.
- It’s a masterclass in accessibility, designed to ensure the art experience isn't gatekept for those who can’t rely on sight alone.

By integrating audio as a core structural element rather than a museum-gift-shop afterthought, Sze is effectively shaming the "please don't touch or breathe near the canvas" crowd. She’s creating environments that invite wonder rather than demanding a PhD in art history to actually enjoy.

It’s a refreshing pivot toward inclusion that feels less like a checklist-driven corporate initiative and more like a genuine evolution of the medium. If you find yourself in any of these three cities, skip the generic tourist traps and the overpriced sourdough.

Go find a Sze installation instead. Your ears, your brain, and your tired eyes will thank you for the change of pace.
