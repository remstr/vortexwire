Rosalía isn’t just playing the pop game anymore; she’s rewriting the entire rulebook in twelve different languages. Her magnum opus, *Lux (Complete Works)*, and its operatic centerpiece "Berghain" have hijacked the top categories, proving the Recording Academy finally has an appetite for Björk-adjacent chaos.

Madonna, however, is likely throwing a very expensive martini at her wall right about now. Despite *Confessions II* being hailed as her sharpest club survey in twenty years, she was unceremoniously ghosted from Album of the Year and even Pop Vocal Album.

The snub list doesn’t end with the Queen of Pop. Noah Kahan also found himself locked out of the big house, proving that even a massive year can’t guarantee you a seat at the main table. 

Meanwhile, the "real music" crowd has pivoted from traditional jazz snobbery to literal fever dreams. Canadian duo Angine de Poitrine—who perform in bizarre, face-covering polka-dot gear—parlayed a viral KEXP session into a Best New Artist nomination.

Lastly, the country music scene has undergone a total estrogen takeover. The Contemporary Country Album ballot is a clean sweep for the women, leaving the Nashville bros staring at a "No Boys Allowed" sign.

- Madonna’s club masterpiece is mostly relegated to the non-televised dance categories.
- Rosalía’s "Berghain" brings German techno-opera and Björk features to the mainstream.
- The Best New Artist slot went to a pair of avant-garde Canadians who look like errant kids' TV characters.
