Lincoln Center’s stage is currently a high-stakes battlefield of artistic legacies. Resident choreographers Lauren Lovette and Robert Battle just dropped their latest works, attempting to carve out fresh territory in a house built by the late, legendary Paul Taylor.

It’s a brutal gig when your landlord is a dead genius whose shadow is long enough to cover most of Midtown. Lovette’s new piece brought an emotional, swinging energy that managed to catch some air, but Battle’s contribution felt more like a beautifully packaged hollow miss.

The issue isn’t a lack of technique—these dancers could probably perform a backflip while filing their taxes. The problem is that whenever the company pivots back to a Taylor original, the new commissions start to look like they’re sprinting through waist-deep water.

- Lovette’s "Echo" offered a genuine emotional pulse that kept the audience leaning in.
- Battle’s work lacked the structural "teeth" needed to stand up to the program's heavy hitters.
- The Taylor classics remain the undisputed champions, proving that some shadows are impossible to escape.

Watching these premieres is a reminder that innovation is hard, but innovating next to a masterpiece is an uphill climb. You can bring all the contemporary flair you want, but Taylor’s choreography has a nasty habit of flattening everything else into the floorboards.

It’s a "thanks for playing" moment for the new guard. Until someone finds a way to disrupt the Taylor DNA, the resident choreographers are just living in a very expensive, very talented shadow.
