If you thought watching Alex Honnold dangle off El Capitan was enough to trigger a panic attack, Jimmy Chin and Chai Vasarhelyi are back to finish the job. Their latest documentary, *Everest: The Other Side*, pivots from Yosemite granite to the thin, frozen air of the mountain’s notorious north side.

While most weekend warriors clog the southern route for a $70k selfie, the north face is where things get genuinely grim. The film tracks a group of climbers who decide that regular oxygen-deprived suffering isn't quite enough; they need the steep, windswept brutality of the Tibetan approach.

- It’s "Free Solo" energy meets "Into Thin Air" anxiety, minus the Hollywood dramatization.
- The cinematography is stunning, provided you like your vistas served with a side of looming frostbite.
- A stark reminder that nature has zero chill—both literally and figuratively.

The directors don’t just capture the ascent; they interrogate the weird, masochistic obsession that drives humans to play chicken with the atmosphere. Chin and Vasarhelyi have mastered the art of making the audience feel every gasp of thin air from the safety of a climate-controlled theater. 

It’s a gorgeous, terrifying look at the literal peak of human ego. At 29,000 feet, the mountain doesn’t care about your redemption arc or your high-tech gear. If you’ve ever wanted to feel small, cold, and utterly mortal, this is your weekend watch.
