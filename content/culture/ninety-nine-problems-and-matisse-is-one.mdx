Alex Katz has spent seven decades making high-society cool look effortless, but apparently, even a 99-year-old icon can get a case of the jitters. The undisputed king of the American "cool" aesthetic is currently facing his toughest critic yet at the Matisse Museum in Nice, and it isn’t some snarky TikTok art reviewer.

Katz is sharing a rare double billing with Henri Matisse, the man who practically invented modern color theory. Despite his own status as a permanent fixture in the MoMA, Katz admitted the pairing gave him serious anxiety, proving that imposter syndrome has no expiration date.

The exhibition pits Katz’s flat, billboard-sized portraits against Matisse’s fluid, light-drenched mastery. It’s a fascinating stylistic standoff between mid-century New York swagger and the sun-soaked elegance of the French Riviera. 

- Katz’s massive canvases from the 1960s are being displayed alongside Matisse’s late-career cut-outs and sketches.
- The artist famously spent his entire career trying to out-paint the European masters; now he’s finally in the room with the ghost of his biggest rival.
- Pro tip: If a centenarian with a legacy of era-defining work still feels underqualified, your LinkedIn profile is probably doing just fine.

While Matisse provides the lyricism, Katz brings the punchy, graphic minimalism that paved the way for Pop Art. It’s a high-stakes dialogue between two titans that suggests even after a century of living, the quest for approval never quite ends.
