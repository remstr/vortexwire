Joe Keery is officially done babysitting teenagers in the 1980s. At least for a season.

The man who turned Steve Harrington into a global icon—largely through exceptional hair volume and a redemption arc for the ages—is heading to Broadway. Keery is set to make his debut in "Other Desert Cities," a move that swaps the Upside Down for the raw intensity of live theater.

While we all know him for his mullet and his psychedelic pop project Djo, Keery isn’t just looking for another massive paycheck. He’s looking for a reset, telling reporters he wants to remember "why I started doing this in the first place."

- **The Stage:** He’s tackling Jon Robin Baitz’s Pulitzer-nominated drama.
- **The Pivot:** It’s a sharp turn from blockbuster CGI to intimate family dysfunction.
- **The Stakes:** No retakes, no green screens, just raw performance.

It’s a gutsy move for an actor who could easily spend the next decade coasting on Netflix residuals and comic-con appearances. Instead, he’s opting for the grueling schedule of eight shows a week in front of a live, judgmental New York audience.

If Keery can survive a pack of Demodogs and the social hierarchy of Hawkins High, he can probably handle a Tuesday night in Manhattan. We just hope the theater’s insurance policy covers that much hairspray.
