Most people assume the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade is a whirlwind of helium and frantic last-minute stitching. In reality, the 100th anniversary march has been in the works since 2021, proving that it takes longer to prep a giant turkey than it does to earn a master's degree.

While the rest of us were still figuring out how to work Zoom, the Macy’s Studio team in New Jersey was already drilling, painting, and stressing over the 2026 milestone. This isn’t just a stroll down Central Park West; it’s a five-year logistics marathon disguised as family-friendly chaos.

- Five years of architectural planning for a single three-hour television broadcast.
- Handcrafted floats that require more structural engineering than your average suburban condo.
- Enough industrial paint and biodegradable glitter to be visible from the International Space Station.

Every float is a handcrafted marvel, built by a dedicated crew who spend their lives worrying about wind speeds and whether a giant Pikachu can successfully navigate a 90-degree turn without a catastrophe. It’s a grueling cycle of carpentry and design that turns a massive warehouse into a Santa-themed fever dream.

By the time the 100th procession actually hits the streets, the organizers will already be three years deep into planning the 105th. It’s a relentless, beautiful grind that keeps this weird, wonderful American tradition afloat—literally and figuratively.
