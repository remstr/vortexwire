Andrey Zvyagintsev, the undisputed king of prestige Russian gloom, has returned with a new feature that suggests modern Russia is a lot like a failing marriage. His latest film, *Minotaur*, turned heads at Cannes by doing what Zvyagintsev does best: making everyone in the room feel deeply uncomfortable about the state of the world.

The story centers on a cheating spouse, but don’t expect a soapy afternoon drama. Instead, the director uses domestic infidelity as a scalpel to dissect the moral rot of a society operating under Putin’s thumb.

- **The Core Conflict:** A messy affair serves as a stand-in for the "small" compromises everyday Russians make to survive an authoritarian regime.
- **The Vibe:** Bleak, beautifully shot, and guaranteed to make you want to drink a very cold vodka in a very dark room.
- **The Director’s Exile:** Having narrowly survived a coma and moved to Europe, Zvyagintsev isn't pulling any punches from his new home.

While Russian state media prefers stories of glorious conquest, *Minotaur* focuses on the internal collapse. It’s a reminder that when a country goes to war with the truth, the first casualty is usually the dinner table conversation.

If you like your cinema with a side of existential crisis and a heavy dose of political subtext, this is the one to watch. Just don't expect a happy ending or a reconciliation.
