Documentaries usually rely on experts in beige offices to explain how a multi-billion dollar lie falls apart. *You Can See Everything* decides that instead of dry facts, you just need to stare directly into Elizabeth Holmes’ soul—or at least her very expensive skin routine.

The film ditches traditional b-roll for extreme, uncomfortably intimate close-ups of its central figures. It’s a high-stakes staring contest with the girlboss of the century, and honestly, you’re probably going to blink first.

- Extreme Close-Ups: The camera lingers so long on Holmes’ face that you start questioning your own reality.
- The Fielder Factor: Nathan Fielder brings his signature "how is this real?" energy to the screen, adding a layer of meta-humor.
- The Billy Evans Study: Seeing Holmes’ husband in such proximity provides a front-row seat to the weirdest domestic bliss ever captured.

This isn’t a deep dive into the technical failures of Theranos; we’ve already had plenty of those. This is a psychological experiment in how much we can learn about a person by watching their pupils dilate in real-time.

The mystery isn't how the machines worked—everyone knows they didn't. The mystery is what’s happening behind those wide eyes while the world burns. It’s funny, it’s terrifying, and it’s the most honest look at a fraudster we’re likely to get.
