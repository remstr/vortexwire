Forget the usual suspects; some serious indie film buzz is coming out of the Himalayas. The 15th Dharamshala International Film Festival (DIFF) is currently lighting up Upper Dharamshala, running from October 29 to November 1, culminating with the India premiere of Shashank Walia's "Birds in the Dark." This debut feature, about a call center exec mistakenly ID'd by a police informer, isn't just closing the show—it's fresh off snagging two prizes at the Busan International Film Festival.

Kicking things off was Rima Das’s "Not a Hero," making its South Asia premiere. DIFF's lineup, curated for "distinctive voices, boundary-pushing filmmaking," truly delivers on its promise of diverse, independent cinema, as programming director Bina Paul highlights.

Highlights from the eclectic program include:
- Varun Grover’s highly anticipated short, "Damned."
- Paromita Vohra’s "Working Girls," illuminating unseen labor across India.
- Mehrdad Oskouei’s "A Fox Under a Pink Moon," following an Afghan artist's poignant journey.
- Yashasvi Juyal’s "The Ink Stained Hand and The Missing Thumb," serving up northern Indian highway existentialism.

Beyond the big screen, DIFF is nurturing emerging talent with its Himalayan Film Fellows initiative. They also bring cinema to local communities, from schools to nunneries, proving truly independent film can thrive anywhere.
