For decades, we’ve projected our collective neuroses onto Miranda Hobbes, the sharp-tongued lawyer who made cynical pragmatism a personality trait. But if you expect Cynthia Nixon to show up to lunch with a dry quip and a skeptical eyebrow, you’re looking at the wrong decade.

Nixon is currently thriving in the corseted drama of The Gilded Age, but her true evolution has happened off-camera. While she’ll always appreciate the woman who made her a household name, the real-life Nixon is far more nuanced—and significantly louder—than her HBO alter ego.

She’s traded the fictional courtroom for the very real political stage, and that’s where the "mellow" actress disappears. Ask her about her kids or her latest role, and you get a warm, veteran performer; ask her about the state of the union, and the fire starts to roar.

- She acknowledges she’ll always be "a Miranda" to the public, but she refuses to let a 90s archetype define her current reality.
- Currently starring as Ada Brook in The Gilded Age, proving she handles a bustle just as well as a power suit.
- Her real passion remains grassroots advocacy, where her famous bluntness is put to much better use than debating Steve’s laundry.

It’s a rare feat to outgrow a character that defined an entire generation’s view of womanhood. Nixon managed it by simply being more interesting than the script.
