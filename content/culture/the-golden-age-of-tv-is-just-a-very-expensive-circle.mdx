Remember when we thought Netflix would liberate us from the cable company’s icy grip? We were young, naive, and clearly didn't see the coming "Plus" apocalypse where every media conglomerate demands $15 a month for the privilege of one good show.

We are living through a legitimate golden age of prestige dragons and high-budget chef travelogues. But the price of admission is no longer just a single subscription; it’s a monthly digital tribute to half a dozen corporate overlords.

The trade-off for infinite choice is a fragmented mess that requires a spreadsheet and a second job to navigate. Here is the fine print on our current "Streaming Revolution":

- Subscription Bloat: You’re paying for five different apps just to watch three shows you actually like.
- The Password Crackdown: Freeloading off your ex’s cousin’s account is officially a dead pastime.
- Ad-Tier Creep: Paying extra to avoid commercials, only for "limited" ads to sneak back in anyway.
- Content Purges: Watching your favorite niche series get scrubbed from existence for a tax write-off.

We wanted freedom from the bundle, but we ended up building a more expensive version of it ourselves. We are now one "innovative" tech breakthrough away from someone inventing a box that puts all these channels in one place for a flat fee.

That’s just cable with better UI. We’ve come full circle, and our bank accounts are the only things not laughing.
