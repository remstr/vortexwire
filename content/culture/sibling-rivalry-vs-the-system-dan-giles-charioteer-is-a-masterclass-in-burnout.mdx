Dan Giles’ new play, Charioteer, isn’t your typical "eat the rich" manifesto. It’s a sharp, intimate look at what happens when two siblings decide they’re done with the rat race and attempt to live entirely outside the gears of modern commerce.

It turns out that deleting your LinkedIn and living on a prayer is significantly harder than it looks on a Pinterest mood board. The play follows a duo who give anticapitalist absolutism the "old college try," pushing their ideals to a logical—and often absurd—extreme.

Giles avoids the usual preachy traps of high-brow political theater. Instead, he delivers a story that feels less like a lecture and more like watching your smartest, most stressed-out friends try to build a utopia in the middle of a systemic breakdown.

- The Vibe: Minimalist staging meets maximalist existential dread.
- The Conflict: Can you truly escape the machine when the machine owns the very ground you’re standing on?
- The Verdict: A lean, mean reminder that "going off the grid" is usually just a fancy way of saying "I no longer have health insurance."

The production manages a delicate balance of dry wit and genuine pathos. Whether you’re a card-carrying socialist or just someone who occasionally wants to throw your MacBook into the Atlantic, there’s a reflection of your own burnout here. 

It’s a tiny play with a massive punch. Charioteer proves the revolution won’t be televised—it’ll probably just be very, very awkward.
