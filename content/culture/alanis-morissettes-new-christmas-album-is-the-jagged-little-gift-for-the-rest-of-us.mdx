Alanis Morissette has spent decades serving as our collective emotional barometer, and she isn’t about to stop just because there’s tinsel on the trees. Her new holiday record, Good Weather for Immaculate Conception, is less "Jingle Bell Rock" and more "Jagged Little Stocking."

Forget the saccharine, department-store sheen of Michael Bublé or Mariah Carey. Morissette is leaning hard into the season’s inherent melancholy, delivering a mix of classics and originals that feel more like a late-night therapy session than a Macy's parade float.

- It’s not exactly a "party" vibe. The title alone suggests a heady mix of theology and atmospheric pressure that only Alanis could pull off.
- Expect those signature vocal runs applied to tracks that acknowledge seasonal depression is just as real as a candy cane.
- The original songs ruminate on the somber side of the solstice, proving you can celebrate a holiday while still feeling like a beautifully complicated mess.

This is the definitive soundtrack for staring out a frosted window while contemplating the existential weight of family gatherings. It’s for anyone who finds the "most wonderful time of the year" to be a little bit draining.

Alanis knows that for the rest of us, the holidays aren’t a sanitized Hallmark movie. If you’d rather have an honest cry into your spiked eggnog than sing about Rudolph, she’s got you covered. This is the Christmas album for people who prefer their joy with a side of introspection.
